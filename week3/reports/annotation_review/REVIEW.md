# Preliminary saved-landmark review

Scope: 15 saved raw, round-1 records from study 317d1b49. No coordinates,
records or tooth registrations were modified. This is a visual screening review,
not expert anatomical validation or a clinical diagnosis.

## Findings

1. **Mesial/distal direction needs correction or confirmation.** Every saved
   record places the mesial CEJ to the image-left of the distal CEJ. Mesial is
   toward the dental midline, not a fixed image direction. The original image
   contains R/L markers. For study teeth 4–6 and 12–15, the CEJ and crest label
   pairs appear reversed relative to the arch midline: mesial should be toward
   image-right at these sites. Tooth 7 is also on that side, but its marked tilt
   requires a separate surface-orientation review. This flags label identity;
   it does not certify any CEJ or crest coordinate.
2. **Tooth 7 requires priority expert review.** It is markedly tilted. The
   individual root-tip and bone-crest boundaries cannot be confidently certified
   from this panoramic image. Confirm eligibility and the shared-apex convention
   before treating it as a reliable research measurement.
3. **Other coordinates are not certified.** The source image has indistinct
   boundaries and overlapping structures. A passed geometric check means the
   crest projects within the CEJ–apex interval; it does not establish that each
   click is anatomically located at the CEJ, crest, or actual apex. Review all
   crest and apex coordinates with an appropriately qualified dental reviewer.

Do not discard or overwrite the original readings. First agree on anatomical
side definitions, landmark instructions and ambiguous-tooth handling. If
corrections are made, retain the originals and record the correction reason.
If only M/D names are exchanged while coordinate pairs remain identical, the
shared-axis worse-side result is unchanged, but surface-specific results change
names. This mathematical symmetry does not validate the point locations.
